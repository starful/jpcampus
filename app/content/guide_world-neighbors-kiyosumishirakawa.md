World Neighbors Kiyosumishirakawa is a modern co-living residence located in Koto Ward, just a few minutes' walk from Kiyosumishirakawa Station on the Toei Oedo and Tokyo Metro Hanzomon lines — two stops from major hubs and roughly 15 minutes to central Tokyo. It targets international students and young professionals who want furnished, flexible housing without the usual key-money burden of traditional Japanese apartments. Below is a full breakdown of costs, amenities, contract terms, and how it stacks up against other options in the neighborhood.

## Location and Neighborhood Fit for Students

Kiyosumishirakawa sits in a quiet, increasingly popular pocket of eastern Tokyo known for its blend of traditional shitamachi charm and a growing café/gallery scene (it's often called Tokyo's "coffee district" thanks to roasters like Blue Bottle's first Japan location). For students, this location offers real practical advantages:

- **Commute**: Direct access to Tokyo Metro Hanzomon Line (reaches Otemachi, Shibuya) and Toei Oedo Line (reaches Iidabashi, Shinjuku, Ryogoku).
- **Proximity to universities**: Reasonable commute (25–40 minutes) to Waseda, Tokyo University of Science (Kagurazaka), and Sophia University campuses; closer still to campuses in the Sumida/Koto area.
- **Daily life**: Supermarkets (Life, Maruetsu), 100-yen shops, and Kiba Park are all within a 10-minute walk, keeping daily costs manageable.
- **Safety**: Koto Ward reports consistently low crime rates compared to nightlife-heavy wards like Shinjuku or Shibuya, which matters for students living alone for the first time.

Compared to share houses clustered around Takadanobaba or Ikebukuro (traditional "gaijin house" territory), Kiyosumishirakawa is quieter and more residential, trading some nightlife convenience for a calmer study environment.

## Monthly Rent and What's Actually Included

World Neighbors properties use an all-inclusive pricing model rather than the itemized rent + utilities + management fee structure common in standard Japanese rentals. At the Kiyosumishirakawa location, pricing generally falls into these tiers:

| Room Type | Monthly Rent (approx.) | Size | Occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared dorm room (2–4 beds) | ¥62,000–¥75,000 | 13–18 m² shared | 2–4 people |
| Private single room | ¥98,000–¥118,000 | 9–13 m² | 1 person |
| Private room w/ ensuite | ¥125,000–¥140,000 | 13–16 m² | 1 person |

What's bundled into that monthly figure:

- Electricity, water, and gas (typically capped with a fair-use policy; excess usage beyond ~¥3,000–5,000/month may incur a small surcharge)
- High-speed Wi-Fi (shared building-wide network, no separate ISP contract needed)
- Furnished room: bed frame, mattress, desk, chair, storage/wardrobe, curtains
- Access to shared kitchen, lounge, laundry room (coin-op, paid per use), and common workspaces
- Basic cleaning of common areas (resident is responsible for private room cleaning)

Unfurnished options are rare at this property — nearly all rooms come furnished, which is a key differentiator from typical share houses where furniture quality and availability vary room to room.

## Initial Move-In Costs: Breaking Down the Numbers

This is where World Neighbors diverges most sharply from traditional Japanese rental contracts, and it's the detail that matters most for students budgeting from abroad.

| Cost Item | Traditional Apartment | World Neighbors Kiyosumishirakawa |
|---|---|---|
| Key money (reikin) | 1 month's rent | ¥0 |
| Deposit (shikikin) | 1–2 months' rent | 1 month's rent (refundable, minus damages) |
| Guarantor company fee | ¥30,000–50,000 | Not required |
| Agency fee | 0.5–1 month's rent | ¥0 (no agent needed) |
| Registration/admin fee | Varies | ¥15,000–20,000 (one-time) |
| First month's rent | 1 month | 1 month (sometimes prorated) |
| Fire insurance | ¥15,000–20,000/2yr | Often included or ¥10,000/year |

**Practical total to move in**: budget roughly ¥180,000–230,000 for a private room (deposit + first month + admin fee + insurance), compared to ¥350,000–450,000+ for a standard Tokyo apartment with key money and a guarantor company. This no-guarantor, no-key-money structure is specifically designed to remove the two biggest barriers foreign students face when renting in Japan — lack of a Japanese guarantor and unfamiliarity with reikin customs.

## Contract Terms for International Students

World Neighbors explicitly markets to the international and student demographic, so the paperwork is lighter than a typical Japanese lease:

- **Minimum stay**: Usually 1 month, though discounted rates kick in for 6-month and 12-month commitments
- **Required documents**: Passport, residence card (Zairyu Card) or proof of visa status, student ID or certificate of enrollment, emergency contact
- **No guarantor required**: The company itself acts as the contracting body, removing the need for a Japanese co-signer — a major pain point for students renting independently for the first time
- **Language support**: Contracts and move-in materials are typically available in English; on-site or remote staff can usually assist in English
- **Cancellation notice**: Standard policy requires 30 days' written notice before move-out; early termination within the first month may forfeit part of the deposit

This flexibility suits exchange students on a single semester as well as degree-seeking students planning a multi-year stay, which is harder to find in standard apartment leasing where 2-year contracts are the norm.

Applicants coming from overseas should note that online applications can often be completed before arriving in Japan, with the deposit and first month paid via international bank transfer — a notable convenience since many traditional landlords require in-person contract signing.

Resident reviews consistently highlight the **community programming** — organized language exchange nights, communal dinners, and co-working sessions in the lounge — as a standout feature versus a plain share house, where social interaction depends entirely on who happens to be living there. Common critiques include **thin walls in older dorm-style rooms** and **limited kitchen space during peak dinner hours** when occupancy is high. Compared to university dormitories, which are often located far from city centers and impose stricter curfews, World Neighbors offers more independence and a more central location, at a somewhat higher price point.

## How to Apply: Step-by-Step

1. **Browse availability** on the World Neighbors website and shortlist a room type that fits your budget and move-in date.
2. **Submit an online inquiry/application** with your passport scan, visa/residence status, and intended move-in date.
3. **Video call or virtual tour** (optional but recommended) to see the actual room and common areas before committing.
4. **Receive and review the digital contract** — confirm the all-inclusive fee structure, cancellation policy, and any fair-use utility caps.
5. **Pay the deposit and first month's rent** via bank transfer or credit card, depending on current payment options.
6. **Confirm move-in logistics** — key pickup method, Wi-Fi login details, and orientation schedule.
7. **Complete ward office registration** within 14 days of arrival for residence card address updates, as required by Japanese immigration law.

For students weighing their first Tokyo housing decision, World Neighbors Kiyosumishirakawa offers a rare combination: a calm, café-filled neighborhood, genuinely all-inclusive pricing, and a contract process built around people who don't have a Japanese guarantor or a long credit history — making it one of the more approachable entry points into Tokyo living for first-time international residents.

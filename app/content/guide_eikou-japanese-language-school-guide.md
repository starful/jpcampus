## What Eikou Japanese Language School Offers and Who It's Right For

Eikou Japanese Language School is a mid-sized, Tokyo-based institution that has built a reputation for structured, exam-focused Japanese instruction aimed at university-bound and career-track international students. If you're comparing language schools in the Tokyo area, the key facts you need up front are these: Eikou offers four main course tracks, tuition runs roughly ¥650,000–¥780,000 per year depending on enrollment season, and the school requires a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application lead time of 3–4 months before your intended start date. Below, we break down every detail — courses, costs, paperwork, campus access, and how Eikou stacks up against nearby alternatives — so you can decide with confidence.

Eikou tends to suit students who want clear placement testing, measurable progress toward JLPT or EJU benchmarks, and a campus within commuting distance of central Tokyo without paying premium rents in Shinjuku or Shibuya. It's less ideal for students seeking a heavily conversation-first, low-homework environment, since Eikou's curriculum leans academic.

## Course Offerings and Level Structure

Eikou organizes its curriculum into placement-tested levels rather than fixed beginner/intermediate labels, which means new students sit a short written and oral test on orientation day before being slotted into one of eight internal levels (N5-equivalent through N1-advanced). The core tracks are:

- **General Japanese Course** — The standard 2-year (4-semester) program, 20 lessons/week, designed for students progressing from zero or near-zero Japanese to JLPT N2/N1 and EJU readiness.
- **University Preparation Course** — Adds dedicated EJU (Examination for Japanese University Admissions) subject tutoring in Japanese, math, and integrated subjects for students targeting undergraduate admission.
- **Business Japanese Course** — A shorter-term (6–12 month) track emphasizing keigo (honorific speech), business email writing, and interview practice for students headed toward employment or specialized vocational schools.
- **Short-Term/Intensive Course** — 3-month blocks for visiting students, working holiday participants, or those topping up conversational skill before a specific test date.

Class sizes are capped at around 15 students, and the school re-tests and potentially re-places students every semester (roughly every 3 months), so motivated students can move up faster than the standard 2-year timeline if they outperform placement expectations.

## Tuition and Fee Schedule

Eikou's pricing is published in yen and billed in stages rather than as one lump sum, which matters for budgeting your COE application and bank statements. Here is the typical breakdown for a first-year General Japanese Course student (prices can shift slightly by intake season, so always confirm the current fee sheet with the admissions office before wiring money):

| Fee Item | Amount (JPY) | When Due |
|---|---|---|
| Application/Screening Fee | ¥20,000 | At document submission |
| Admission Fee (one-time) | ¥60,000 | Upon acceptance |
| Tuition (per year) | ¥600,000–¥680,000 | Split into 1–2 installments |
| Teaching Materials Fee | ¥25,000–¥35,000/year | With first tuition payment |
| Facility/Maintenance Fee | ¥20,000/year | With tuition |
| Health Insurance Guidance Fee | ¥5,000 (one-time) | On arrival orientation |

**Estimated first-year total: roughly ¥730,000–¥820,000**, not including your own living costs, dormitory or apartment deposit, or the National Health Insurance premium you'll pay once registered as a resident.

A few budgeting notes that matter in practice:

- Tuition for year two is typically due about one month before the start of the next academic term, so plan your remittances or part-time work income accordingly.
- Students who withdraw after the COE is issued but before arrival may forfeit the admission fee — refund policies on tuition itself vary by how far into the term you withdraw, so get the refund clause in writing.
- Scholarship or tuition-reduction slots are limited and usually awarded based on attendance and test scores from your first semester, not at initial enrollment.

## Application Timeline and Required Documents

Because Eikou sponsors the Certificate of Eligibility for most overseas applicants, the timeline is longer than a simple "apply and enroll" process. Immigration processing is the long pole here, not the school's internal review.

**Typical intake months:** April, July, October, and January — April is the largest and most competitive intake, so apply early if that's your target.

Recommended timeline for an April start:

1. **November–December (prior year):** Submit application packet to Eikou's admissions office.
2. **December–January:** School reviews documents internally and submits COE application to the regional Immigration Bureau.
3. **January–March:** Immigration processes the COE (this step alone can take 1–3 months and is outside the school's control).
4. **March:** COE issued, sent to student; student applies for Student Visa at the nearest Japanese embassy/consulate.
5. **Late March:** Visa issued; student finalizes flights and housing.
6. **Early April:** Orientation and placement testing begin.

### Required Documents Checklist

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Completed application form | Provided by Eikou, filled in applicant's hand |
| Passport copy | All pages with personal information |
| Certificate of graduation (or expected graduation) | From high school or university, notarized if not in English/Japanese |
| Academic transcripts | Last 2 years minimum |
| Certificate of bank balance | Usually must show funds equivalent to one year of tuition + living costs |
| Statement of financial support (sponsor's affidavit) | Required if a parent/relative is funding study |
| Sponsor's income/employment certificate | Tax statement or employment proof |
| Passport-size photos | Typically 4–6, specific size required |
| Japanese language study history (if any) | Hours studied, textbook used, certificates |

Missing or inconsistent financial documentation is the single most common reason COE applications get delayed or rejected at the Immigration Bureau stage, so double-check that the sponsor's name, relationship, and bank balance figures are consistent across every document before submitting.

## Campus Location and Daily Access

Eikou's main campus sits in a residential-commercial pocket of Tokyo, chosen deliberately to keep rent (and therefore tuition) lower than schools located directly in Shinjuku or Ikebukuro, while still staying within a single train transfer of major hubs.

- **Nearest station:** roughly a 5–8 minute walk from the closest JR or subway station, depending on which campus building you're assigned to for your course level.
- **Commute to Shinjuku:** approximately 20–25 minutes by train.
- **Commute to Ikebukuro:** approximately 15–20 minutes.
- **Nearby conveniences:** supermarkets, 100-yen stores, and budget eateries within a 10-minute walk — important for students self-catering on a tight monthly budget.
- **Dormitory options:** Eikou partners with 2–3 nearby dormitory operators rather than running dorms directly, with monthly rent typically in the ¥45,000–¥65,000 range for a shared or compact single room, utilities often billed separately.

For students not using school-arranged housing, the surrounding neighborhood has a reasonable supply of share-houses and small apartments in a similar price band, though availability tightens right before April and October intakes — start your housing search as soon as your COE is issued, not after you land.

## How Eikou Compares to Nearby Competing Schools

Tokyo has no shortage of language schools, and several sit within the same general catchment area as Eikou, competing for the same applicant pool. The honest comparison comes down to academic intensity, cost, and student support style rather than any single school being objectively "best."

| Factor | Eikou | Typical Nearby Competitor A (exam-focused, similar price tier) | Typical Nearby Competitor B (conversation-focused, premium location) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual tuition | ¥600,000–¥680,000 | ¥620,000–¥700,000 | ¥750,000–¥850,000 |
| Class size | ~15 students | ~18–20 students | ~10–12 students |
| EJU/university prep support | Strong, dedicated track | Moderate, add-on tutoring | Limited/none |
| Location premium | Lower (residential area) | Comparable | Higher (central ward) |
| Placement re-testing frequency | Every semester | Once a year | Every semester |
| Dormitory arrangement | Partnered external dorms | Partnered external dorms | On-campus dorm available |
| Homework intensity | Moderate–Heavy | Heavy | Light |

**Practical takeaway:** if your priority is getting into a Japanese university and you want structured EJU coaching without paying central-Tokyo rent premiums, Eikou's balance of cost and academic rigor is competitive. If you specifically want on-campus housing or a lighter, conversation-first pace, the premium-location competitor may justify its higher price. If you're purely cost-sensitive and don't need EJU support, Competitor A's slightly larger class sizes come with marginally lower long-run costs but less individualized exam coaching.

## Making Your Final Decision on Timing and Budget

Before you commit, run your own numbers against two things: your realistic intake deadline and your full first-year budget including non-tuition costs. Apply for the April intake no later than November of the prior year if you want real breathing room for COE delays. Budget for roughly ¥900,000–¥1,000,000 in total first-year costs once you add dormitory rent, insurance, and daily living expenses on top of Eikou's published tuition figures — schools rarely advertise that fuller number, but it's the one that actually determines whether your first year goes smoothly.

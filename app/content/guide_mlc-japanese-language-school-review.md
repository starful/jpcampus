## What Is MLC and Who Is It Really For?

MLC Japanese Language School (officially MLC Japanese Language School, based in Shin-Okubo, Tokyo) is a mid-sized private institution that has built its reputation on structured exam-prep courses and a tight academic schedule. If you're searching for a review of MLC, you're probably trying to answer three things: how much it actually costs once all the fees are added up, whether the course track fits your goals (university entrance vs. general fluency vs. JLPT), and what day-to-day life looks like once you're enrolled. This guide walks through all three with real numbers and comparisons, so you can decide whether MLC is the right fit before you commit a non-refundable application fee.

MLC is best suited for students who want a no-nonsense, test-focused environment — particularly those targeting EJU (Examination for Japanese University Admissions) or JLPT N2/N1 within a one-to-two-year window. It's less ideal for students who want a relaxed, conversation-heavy, or highly international social scene, since the school leans academic rather than lifestyle-oriented.

## Tuition and Fee Breakdown at MLC

MLC's pricing follows the standard Japanese language school structure: a one-time application fee, an entrance fee, and then tuition billed per term (usually per 3-month or 6-month block, depending on the course length you enroll in). Here's an approximate breakdown based on a standard 1-year general course:

| Fee Type | Approximate Cost (JPY) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Application/Screening Fee | ¥20,000–¥25,000 | Paid when submitting documents; non-refundable |
| Entrance Fee (入学金) | ¥30,000–¥50,000 | One-time, charged only in your first term |
| Tuition (per year) | ¥660,000–¥720,000 | Varies by course length and intake month |
| Textbook/Material Fee | ¥15,000–¥25,000/year | Billed separately from tuition |
| Facility Fee | ¥20,000–¥30,000/year | Covers building maintenance, Wi-Fi, etc. |
| **Estimated Total (Year 1)** | **¥745,000–¥850,000** | Before housing and living costs |

A few things worth knowing before you budget:

- **Intake timing matters.** April and October intakes tend to have the most seat availability and the most predictable fee schedules; January and July starts sometimes carry slightly different pro-rated tuition.
- **Fees are rarely refundable past the application stage**, so don't pay the entrance fee until your visa eligibility has been informally confirmed by the school's admissions staff.
- **Tuition does not include the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) processing time**, which can take 1–3 months and is handled jointly by the school and immigration.
- Short-term (3-month) courses are available but priced less efficiently per month than the 1-year package — expect to pay roughly ¥230,000–¥260,000 for a single 3-month term if you're not committing long-term.

## Course Structure: General Japanese, Exam-Prep, and Specialized Tracks

MLC splits its curriculum into a handful of tracks rather than a single generic pipeline, which is one of its stronger selling points.

**General Japanese Course**
This is the default track for most new students, split into levels roughly aligned with JLPT N5 through N1. Classes run Monday–Friday, typically either a morning (9:00–12:30) or afternoon (13:00–16:30) shift, with 4 class periods a day. Class sizes average 15–20 students, grouped by placement test results rather than nationality or age.

**EJU / University Entrance Prep**
This track is MLC's flagship offering and the reason many students choose the school over competitors. It layers EJU-specific subjects (Japanese as a foreign subject, plus optional math/science modules) on top of regular language classes once students reach roughly N3–N2 level. Teachers provide past-paper drilling, mock exams, and one-on-one guidance for university application essays and interviews.

**JLPT Intensive Course**
Offered as a supplementary or seasonal module (often condensed in the 2 months before the July and December JLPT sittings), this focuses purely on grammar drilling, listening speed, and reading comprehension tactics rather than conversational skill-building.

**Business Japanese / Conversation Add-ons**
These exist but are smaller and less emphasized — don't expect the robust business-Japanese ecosystem you'd find at schools like ARC Academy or ISI.

| Course Track | Typical Duration | Best For | Class Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Japanese | 3 months–2 years | Beginners to N2 learners | Grammar, vocab, all 4 skills |
| EJU Prep | 6 months–1.5 years | University-bound students | Exam strategy, academic writing |
| JLPT Intensive | 2–3 months (seasonal) | N2/N1 test-takers | Drilling, mock tests |
| Business/Conversation | 3–6 months | Working professionals | Speaking fluency, keigo |

## Campus Location in Shin-Okubo and the Daily Commute

MLC's main campus sits in Shin-Okubo, one of Tokyo's most student-dense neighborhoods and also home to the city's largest Koreatown. This location has real practical upsides:

- **Transit access:** Shin-Okubo Station (JR Yamanote Line) is roughly a 5–7 minute walk from campus, putting Shinjuku — one of Tokyo's biggest transit and job hubs — just one stop away (about 2 minutes by train).
- **Cost of living nearby:** Because the area is saturated with language schools, there's a dense supply of student-friendly apartments, cheap Korean and Japanese eateries, and 24-hour supermarkets, which keeps daily costs manageable compared to more central wards like Shibuya or Minato.
- **Commute reality for students in outer wards:** Many students don't live in Shin-Okubo itself but commute from areas like Itabashi, Nerima, or Kita ward, where rent is 20–30% cheaper. Expect a 20–40 minute one-way commute if you choose a cheaper apartment further out.
- **Noise and density trade-off:** Shin-Okubo is lively but can feel crowded and noisy in the evenings, particularly around the main shopping street — worth knowing if you're sensitive to that kind of environment.

## Dorm and Housing Options Near MLC

MLC does not operate its own dormitory network the way some larger chains (like Genki Japanese School or Naganuma School) do. Instead, it partners with third-party dormitory operators and also helps students find private apartments through affiliated real estate agencies. Here's what the typical options look like:

| Housing Type | Monthly Cost (JPY) | Deposit/Key Money | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner dormitory (shared room) | ¥45,000–¥60,000 | Usually 1 month rent | Includes utilities, closest to campus |
| Partner dormitory (private room) | ¥65,000–¥85,000 | 1 month rent | Limited availability, book early |
| Private apartment (share house) | ¥50,000–¥70,000 | 1–2 months | More independence, shared kitchen/bath |
| Private studio apartment | ¥70,000–¥95,000 | 2–5 months (deposit + key money) | Requires guarantor, harder for new arrivals |

Practical notes on housing:

- Dormitory spots fill up fast for April and October intakes — apply for housing at the same time as your enrollment, not after you receive your COE.
- Most dorms require a separate dorm contract and fee, paid independently of tuition, so factor it into your total first-year budget.
- If you're arranging a private apartment without school assistance, you'll likely need a Japanese guarantor or a guarantor company (adds roughly ¥20,000–¥40,000 in extra setup fees).

## Student Reviews: What People Actually Say

Feedback on MLC tends to cluster around a few recurring themes across forums, Google reviews, and student blogs:

**Positive feedback:**
- Strong, structured EJU prep — multiple reviewers credit MLC's mock exams and essay coaching for getting them into mid-tier and some top-tier Japanese universities.
- Teachers are described as strict but responsive, especially for exam-track students who need individualized feedback.
- Shin-Okubo location is consistently praised for convenience and affordability relative to schools based in central Tokyo wards.

**Common criticisms:**
- Class sizes in the general track can feel large and fast-paced for true beginners who need more speaking practice.
- The social/extracurricular side is thinner than at schools that market themselves around student life (campus trips, clubs, cultural events are less frequent).
- A handful of reviewers mention that academic pressure is noticeably higher than at "lifestyle-first" schools, which is a plus for exam-focused students but a mismatch for casual learners.

The overall pattern: students who enroll specifically for EJU or JLPT preparation tend to leave satisfied, while students hoping for a more relaxed, immersion-and-friendship-focused year sometimes find MLC too rigid for their taste.

## How MLC Pricing Compares to Other Tokyo Schools

To put MLC's cost in context, here's how its first-year total stacks up against a few other well-known Tokyo language schools offering comparable EJU/general tracks:

| School | Location | Approx. 1st-Year Total (JPY) | Strongest For |
|---|---|---|---|
| MLC Japanese Language School | Shin-Okubo | ¥745,000–¥850,000 | EJU/university prep |
| ARC Academy | Shinjuku/Ikebukuro | ¥800,000–¥900,000 | Business Japanese, career support |
| ISI Language School | Shinjuku/Takadanobaba | ¥850,000–¥950,000 | Broad course variety, strong support staff |
| Genki Japanese School | Takadanobaba | ¥750,000–¥820,000 | Casual learners, dorm convenience |
| Naganuma School | Shibuya | ¥900,000–¥1,000,000 | Long-established academic reputation |

MLC sits in the lower-to-middle range of Tokyo tuition costs, which — combined with Shin-Okubo's relatively affordable housing market — makes it one of the better value options for students prioritizing exam results over campus amenities. If your budget is tight and your main goal is passing EJU or JLPT N2/N1 to get into a Japanese university, MLC's price-to-outcome ratio is genuinely competitive. If you're looking for a bigger social scene, more elective variety, or a dedicated dormitory network, you may get better value from a school like Genki or ISI even at a similar or slightly higher price point.

**Bottom line:** MLC is a solid, no-frills choice for serious exam-track students who want an affordable Shin-Okubo base and don't mind a more academically demanding daily schedule. Budget roughly ¥750,000–¥850,000 for your first year in tuition and fees alone, plus ¥50,000–¥85,000/month for housing depending on whether you choose a partner dormitory or private apartment.

## What You'll Actually Pay to Move Into Sakura House Kagurazaka

Sakura House's draw for international students is simple: no guarantor, no key money, and furnished rooms that let you skip Japan's notoriously paperwork-heavy rental process. The Kagurazaka branch (officially listed under Sakura House's "Kagurazaka" and nearby "Edogawabashi" properties) follows the company-wide fee structure, but the actual yen amount shifts with room type. Here's the realistic breakdown based on current Sakura House pricing for this property.

| Fee | Typical Amount | When You Pay It |
|---|---|---|
| Registration fee | ¥10,500 (one-time, non-refundable) | At booking |
| Deposit | 1x monthly rent (refundable, minus cleaning) | Before move-in |
| Key money / "reikin" | ¥0 — Sakura House doesn't charge this | N/A |
| Agency fee | ¥0 — no outside agent involved | N/A |
| Utilities (electricity, water, gas, internet) | Included in rent at most Kagurazaka rooms | Monthly, bundled |
| First month's rent | ¥85,000–¥145,000 depending on room type | Before move-in |
| Guarantor fee | ¥0 — Sakura House acts as guarantor | N/A |

The headline advantage over a standard Japanese apartment is obvious: a typical private rental near Kagurazaka Station demands key money (1 month), agency fee (1 month), deposit (1–2 months), and a guarantor company fee (¥30,000–¥60,000) — often ¥250,000+ before you've paid a single month's rent. Sakura House compresses that down to roughly deposit + first month + the flat registration fee, which is why so many exchange students and first-time movers to Tokyo gravitate toward it.

One caveat residents flag repeatedly: the deposit refund at move-out is where disputes happen. Sakura House deducts a standard cleaning fee (often ¥15,000–¥30,000 depending on room size) automatically, and any damage beyond normal wear can eat further into it. Photograph your room thoroughly on move-in day — more on that below.

## The Move-In Timeline: From Online Booking to Keys in Hand

Sakura House markets itself on speed, and for the Kagurazaka branch, residents consistently confirm that the process is genuinely faster than applying for private housing — but it's not instant.

**Typical timeline:**

1. **Day 0 — Online inquiry.** You submit a booking request through Sakura House's website, specifying Kagurazaka or a nearby sister property as your preference.
2. **Day 1–3 — Availability confirmation.** Staff reply (usually in English) confirming room availability and sending a provisional hold. Popular room types at Kagurazaka — especially single rooms under ¥100,000 — can be booked out weeks ahead during the March–April and September intake rushes.
3. **Day 3–7 — Document submission.** You send a passport scan and, if applicable, your student visa / Certificate of Eligibility or residence card. No income proof, no Japanese guarantor, no hanko required — this is the single biggest contrast with standard rentals.
4. **Day 7–10 — Payment.** Registration fee, deposit, and first month's rent are wired or paid by card before you receive a confirmed move-in date.
5. **Move-in day.** You check in at the Sakura House Kagurazaka reception desk (not a remote key-box handoff, which residents appreciate), get a room walkthrough, and receive your key and resident handbook.

**Reality check from reviews:** several residents note that if you're applying from overseas, factor in 2–3 weeks minimum between first inquiry and move-in once you include visa logistics and international payment processing. Those already in Japan on a tourist visa or switching from another dorm report turnarounds as short as 5–7 days.

## Inside the Kagurazaka Dorm: Room Types and What Daily Living Feels Like

Kagurazaka operates as a share-house/dorm hybrid rather than a single-building tower like some of Sakura House's larger properties. Rooms range from compact singles to shared twins, with communal kitchens and lounges on each floor.

| Room Type | Approx. Monthly Rent | Shared Facilities | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single room (standard) | ¥95,000–¥125,000 | Shared kitchen, bathroom, lounge | Students wanting privacy |
| Single room (compact/economy) | ¥75,000–¥90,000 | Shared kitchen, bathroom | Budget-conscious long stays |
| Twin/shared room | ¥65,000–¥80,000 per person | Shared kitchen, bathroom, lounge | Friends moving together, short-term |
| Studio (en-suite) | ¥140,000+ | Private bathroom, shared kitchen | Students prioritizing privacy over cost |

Resident feedback on the living experience is fairly consistent across review platforms and student forums:

- **Kitchens get busy.** Multiple residents mention that shared kitchens on the more popular floors are crowded around 7–9pm, and a few bring their own small appliances (rice cooker, kettle) to avoid the queue.
- **Cleaning rotations work, mostly.** Common areas are cleaned by staff weekly, but residents say bathroom cleanliness varies by how considerate your floor-mates are — a common theme in any share-house, not unique to Kagurazaka.
- **Insulation and heating are dated in older rooms.** A recurring winter complaint: some Kagurazaka units are in older buildings, and air conditioning units double as the only heat source. Residents recommend requesting a room tour (virtual, if booking from abroad) before committing to a specific unit.
- **Wi-Fi is solid but shared.** Internet is included, and most reviews rate it as reliable for video calls, though a few mention slowdowns during peak evening hours when many residents are streaming simultaneously.

## Getting Around: What Residents Say About Kagurazaka's Transport Links

This is where Kagurazaka consistently scores well in resident reviews, and it's a legitimate selling point versus Sakura House's outer-Tokyo branches.

- **Kagurazaka Station (Tozai Line)** is roughly a 5–8 minute walk from most Sakura House units in the area, putting you on a direct line to Otemachi, Nihonbashi, and Nishi-Funabashi — useful for students commuting to universities or part-time jobs in central business districts.
- **Iidabashi Station** (Tozai, Yurakucho, Namboku, Oedo lines, plus JR Sobu) is about 10–12 minutes on foot, giving residents a genuine multi-line hub within walking distance — repeatedly cited in reviews as the practical reason to choose this branch over similarly priced options in less-connected neighborhoods.
- **Edogawabashi Station (Yurakucho Line)** serves as a closer option for some of the Sakura House units technically listed under the Kagurazaka cluster.

Residents studying at Sophia University, Waseda, or language schools near Takadanobaba frequently mention commute times of 15–25 minutes door-to-door, which they rate favorably compared to friends living in Sakura House's Ikebukuro or Nakano branches.

## Safety and Neighborhood Character After Dark

Kagurazaka's reputation as a quiet, somewhat upscale residential pocket of Tokyo — historically known for its French expat community and traditional stone-paved streets — carries through directly into resident safety reviews.

- Multiple reviewers, including women living alone, describe walking back from the station after midnight as comfortable, citing good street lighting on the main approach roads and a visible, if low-key, local police presence (a koban near the station).
- The area's reputation for small restaurants, cafés, and a older, quieter demographic (compared to nightlife-heavy areas like Shinjuku or Shibuya) is cited as a deliberate reason some students choose it — less noise, fewer late-night disturbances near the dorm itself.
- A minority of reviews flag the hilly, narrow backstreets as poorly lit once you move off the main thoroughfares, and recommend sticking to the station-facing route at night, particularly in winter when it gets dark early.
- Petty theft and bike-related incidents are mentioned as rare rather than absent — standard advice from residents is the same as anywhere in Tokyo: register your bicycle, don't leave valuables visible in communal areas.

## The Resident Verdict

Pulled together, the pattern in Kagurazaka reviews is remarkably consistent: people choose it for the walkable, multi-line transport access and the calmer, safer residential feel, and they tolerate the trade-offs of shared-kitchen dorm living and occasionally dated room conditions because the cost-to-location ratio is hard to beat in central Tokyo. If you're weighing this against Shinjuku-area convenience or Nakano-area affordability, Kagurazaka lands as the pick for students who want proximity to central Tokyo without the constant noise — provided you go in clear-eyed about move-in timing, the deposit deduction process, and booking your preferred room type early during peak intake seasons.

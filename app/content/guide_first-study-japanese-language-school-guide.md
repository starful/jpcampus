## What First Study Japanese Language School Actually Offers

First Study Japanese Language School is a mid-sized institution built specifically for international students who want a structured, measurable path through Japanese language study — whether that path ends in a Japanese university, a vocational school, or a corporate job in Japan. Unlike schools that offer a single generic curriculum, First Study runs three distinct course tracks (General Japanese, EJU Preparation, and Business Japanese), each with its own pacing, textbooks, and outcome goals. If you're comparing schools right now, the two things you need clarity on before applying are: which track matches your post-graduation goal, and how the tuition is actually billed across terms (not just the headline "per year" number schools advertise). This guide breaks down both, plus the admission paperwork, campus facilities, and what current students say about daily life there.

## Course Tracks: Choosing Between General, EJU-Prep, and Business

First Study divides students into three tracks starting from the second term, after an initial placement test establishes your baseline level (N5–N1 equivalent).

**General Japanese Track**
Designed for students who want solid conversational and written fluency without a fixed exam deadline. Classes run four periods a day, five days a week, with rotating focus on grammar, kanji, listening, and speaking. This is the most flexible track and the one most students default into if they haven't yet decided on university vs. employment.

**EJU Preparation Track**
Built around the Examination for Japanese University Admissions (EJU) schedule, held every June and November. From intermediate level onward, this track adds dedicated blocks for EJU-format Japanese, plus optional subject tutoring in Mathematics, Science, and Japan & the World (the EJU's social studies component). Mock exams are run roughly every six weeks so students can track score trajectory before the real test.

**Business Japanese Track**
Aimed at students targeting employment at Japanese companies after graduation (the "shushoku katsudo" path). This track layers in keigo (honorific language), business email writing, phone etiquette, and mock interview practice from upper-intermediate level. It's the smallest of the three tracks by enrollment but has the highest reported job-placement satisfaction among students surveyed.

| Track | Best For | Weekly Hours | Key Add-Ons |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Japanese | Undecided / conversational goals | 20 hrs | Elective culture workshops |
| EJU Preparation | University-bound applicants | 22 hrs | Mock EJU exams, subject tutoring |
| Business Japanese | Job-seekers post-graduation | 20 hrs | Keigo training, interview practice |

Switching tracks mid-year is allowed once per academic year with written request to the academic office, but it's discouraged after the EJU June sitting since it disrupts exam prep continuity.

## Tuition Breakdown by Term

First Study bills in four-term cycles (April, July, October, January starts), and the published "annual tuition" figure can be misleading if you're only enrolling for part of the year. Here's how costs actually break down, in Japanese yen, based on the school's published 2026 fee schedule:

| Fee Item | 1 Term (3 months) | 2 Terms (6 months) | 4 Terms (12 months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Admission Fee (one-time) | ¥30,000 | ¥30,000 | ¥30,000 |
| Enrollment/Application Fee | ¥20,000 | ¥20,000 | ¥20,000 |
| Tuition | ¥180,000 | ¥345,000 | ¥660,000 |
| Facility Fee | ¥15,000 | ¥28,000 | ¥50,000 |
| Textbook & Materials | ¥12,000 | ¥20,000 | ¥35,000 |
| **Estimated Total** | **¥257,000** | **¥443,000** | **¥795,000** |

A few notes that matter when budgeting:

- The admission and application fees are charged **once**, regardless of how many terms you enroll for — they are not per-term.
- EJU Prep and Business tracks carry a supplementary materials fee of roughly ¥8,000–¥15,000 per term for track-specific workbooks (mock EJU test booklets, business correspondence guides).
- Tuition does not include the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application support fee, which First Study charges separately at ¥15,000 for first-time student visa applicants.
- Students who commit to a full 4-term (1-year) contract upfront receive roughly an 8–10% discount versus paying term-by-term, which is why most long-term students front-load payment if finances allow.
- Refund policy: withdrawing before the term starts refunds tuition minus a ¥20,000 administrative charge; withdrawing after classes begin forfeits that term's tuition entirely but unused future terms are refundable.

## Admission Requirements and Application Timeline

First Study accepts new students for the April, July, October, and January intakes, though April and October are the primary intakes with the widest course availability. The application window typically opens 5–6 months before each intake, so for an October 2027 start, you'd want documents submitted by roughly April–May 2027.

Required documents for first-time applicants:

1. Completed application form (provided by the school)
2. Final academic transcript and graduation certificate (high school minimum; notarized translation if not in English or Japanese)
3. Certificate of enrollment or graduation from your most recent institution
4. Passport copy
5. Financial sponsor documentation — bank balance certificate (typically requiring the equivalent of ¥2,000,000+ on deposit) and sponsor's income/tax statement
6. Sponsor's relationship proof (family register, birth certificate, or notarized affidavit if sponsor is not a parent)
7. Study plan essay in Japanese or English explaining your motivation and post-graduation goals
8. Two passport-style photos

Once documents are reviewed and accepted, First Study submits the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application to the regional Immigration Services Agency on the student's behalf. COE processing generally takes 1–3 months, after which the student applies for the student visa at the nearest Japanese embassy or consulate. Realistically, budget at least 6 months total from first document submission to arrival in Japan — starting late is the single most common reason applicants miss their intended intake.

## Campus Facilities and Daily Environment

First Study's main campus is a mid-rise building with classrooms capped at roughly 15–18 students per class, which keeps the student-to-teacher speaking ratio favorable compared to larger chain schools. Facilities include:

- A self-study lounge with kanji drill terminals and past EJU exam archives, open until 8 PM on weekdays
- A small library stocked with JLPT and EJU reference materials in multiple languages
- A student lounge/kitchenette area where students frequently form study groups across nationalities
- On-site academic counseling office handling track changes, university application letters of recommendation, and visa renewal paperwork
- A part-time job (baito) support bulletin board, since most student visas cap part-time work at 28 hours/week

The school does not offer dormitories directly but maintains a vetted list of partner share-houses and apartment agencies, with move-in support typically arranged in the two weeks before term start. Rent through partner housing generally runs ¥45,000–¥70,000/month depending on distance from campus and whether it's a shared or private room.

## Student Feedback: What Current and Former Students Say

Feedback compiled from student forums, alumni interviews, and exit surveys tends to cluster around a few consistent themes:

**Strengths most frequently mentioned:**
- Teachers are responsive to individual pacing issues, especially in EJU-prep where mock test scores are reviewed one-on-one
- Business track graduates report feeling genuinely prepared for job interviews, specifically citing the keigo and mock interview drills as the most useful part of the curriculum
- Class sizes feel manageable compared to larger competitor schools with 25+ student classrooms

**Recurring criticisms:**
- General track students sometimes feel the pacing is too slow if they already have strong self-study habits, since the track isn't built around a fixed exam deadline
- A few students noted the facility fee and materials fee aren't always clearly itemized upfront in initial marketing materials, leading to minor billing confusion at enrollment
- Housing support, while available, is described as "assistance" rather than guaranteed placement — students who apply late sometimes scramble for housing in their first few weeks

|---|---|---|
| Teacher responsiveness | High | Positive |
| Business track job prep | Moderate | Strongly positive |
| General track pacing | Moderate | Mixed |
| Fee transparency at enrollment | Low-Moderate | Negative |
| Housing placement speed | Low | Mixed |

## Making the Right Track and Budget Decision

If your goal is university admission, the EJU Preparation track's structured mock exams justify its slightly higher weekly hour commitment — don't default to General track just because it's the "safe" option if you already know you're university-bound. If you're aiming for employment, the Business track's keigo and interview training is difficult to replicate through self-study, making it worth the track switch even if you started in General. Budget-wise, commit to the full 4-term contract only if your financial sponsor can comfortably front ¥795,000+ at once; otherwise, the per-term flexibility — despite the smaller discount — protects you against forfeiting a full term's tuition if personal circumstances change. Whichever track you choose, start your document collection at least six months before your target intake, since COE processing delays are the most common reason applicants lose a full term's placement.
